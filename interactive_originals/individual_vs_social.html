Individual Choice vs. Social Production

An individual faces a linear budget constraint (constant opportunity cost), while society faces a bowed-out PPF (increasing opportunity cost)

A. Budget Constraint vs. PPF
Explanation

Panel (a) shows an individual opportunity set—a budget constraint. Given fixed income and prices, a consumer can afford any combination of Good 1 and Good 2 on or below this line. The trade-off is linear because prices are constant: each additional unit of Good 1 always costs the same amount of Good 2 foregone. The slope equals the price ratio \( -P_1/P_2 \), and it does not change as you move along the line.

Panel (b) shows a social production possibilities frontier. When an entire economy allocates resources between Good 1 and Good 2, the trade-off is typically not constant. The PPF bows outward because of increasing opportunity costs: as society produces more of one good, it must pull resources that are progressively less suited to that good and more suited to the other, making each additional unit costlier than the last.

The contrast is fundamental. An individual takes market prices as given, so their constraint is a straight line. Society, however, determines the overall allocation, and the concavity of the PPF reflects the heterogeneity of resources—not all workers, land, or capital are equally productive in every use. This distinction between the individual's linear world and society's curved frontier is central to understanding why market prices emerge and why specialization benefits the economy.
