Factors That Shift Demand

Non-price determinants that cause the entire demand curve to move right (increase) or left (decrease)

Demand Shift Factors
Explanation

A demand curve shifts when a non-price determinant of demand changes. When a factor makes consumers want to buy more at every price, the entire curve shifts rightward (D₀ → D₁). When it makes them want less, the curve shifts leftward (D₀ → D₁).

Six key shifters: (1) Tastes & preferences — greater popularity shifts right. (2) Number of buyers — more buyers, more demand. (3) Income — for normal goods, higher income shifts right. (4) Price of substitutes — when a competing good gets pricier, demand for this good rises. (5) Price of complements — when a complementary good gets cheaper, demand rises. (6) Future expectations — expecting higher prices or scarcity encourages buying now.

Each factor works symmetrically: the opposite change shifts demand leftward. The shift amount slider controls how far apart D₀ and D₁ are, and the curvature slider controls the concavity of the curves.
