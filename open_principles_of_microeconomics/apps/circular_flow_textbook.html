The Circular Flow Diagram

Households and firms interact in the goods and services market and in the labor market. Drag the boxes and labels to rearrange.

A. Households and Firms
Explanation

The circular flow diagram pictures the economy as two groups, households and firms, that interact in two markets: the goods and services market, in which firms sell and households buy, and the labor market, in which households sell labor to firms.

Firms produce and sell goods and services to households (arrow A). Households pay for those goods and services, and the payments become the firms' revenue (arrow B). Arrows A and B are the two sides of the goods and services market. Where do households get the income to pay? They provide the labor and other resources firms need (arrow C), and firms pay for them with wages, salaries and benefits (arrow D). Arrows C and D are the two sides of the labor market.

The direction of the arrows is the point: everything on the top half flows to households, everything on the bottom half flows to firms, and each group's spending is the other group's income.
