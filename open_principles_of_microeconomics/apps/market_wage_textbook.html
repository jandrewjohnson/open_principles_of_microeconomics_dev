The Market Wage Rate

Adding up every firm's demand and every worker's supply gives the wage and the number of jobs the market settles on

A. Supply and Demand for Labor
Explanation

The market demand for labor is the horizontal sum of what every firm wants to hire at each wage, and it slopes down. The market supply is the horizontal sum of what every worker is willing to offer, and it slopes up: a higher wage in one market draws people in from other jobs and other places.

Where the two meet is the going wage Wmarket and the number of jobs Lmarket. This is the wage an individual firm takes as given in the previous figures. The sliders shift each curve, so you can see what raises the wage and what raises employment.
