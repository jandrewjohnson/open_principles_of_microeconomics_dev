Corporate Profits After Tax

Profits climbed through the 2000s, collapsed in late 2008, recovered within two years and went on to new highs

A. Corporate Profits, 2000 to 2015
Explanation

These are the after-tax profits of American corporations, adjusted for inventory and capital consumption. They roughly tripled between 2000 and 2007, fell off a cliff in late 2008 as the financial crisis hit, and were back above their pre-recession level within about two years.

Profits matter here because they are one of the two ways a firm can finance investment: spend its own earnings, or raise money from outside. A firm with profits this size can fund a great deal internally, which is why the chapter opens with them before turning to borrowing and issuing shares. The slider reads the series at any point; the values are sampled from the book's own figure.
