The Circular Flow Diagram

A. Households, Firms and Two Markets
Explanation

The circular flow diagram pictures the economy as two groups, households and firms, that meet in two markets. In the goods and services market (top) firms sell and households buy. In the labor market, or market for factors of production (bottom), households sell labor and other resources and firms buy them.

Firms produce and sell goods and services to households: arrow A. Households pay for them, and those payments become the firms' revenue: arrow B. Where do households get the money? They supply labor and other inputs to firms: arrow C. Firms pay for those inputs with wages, salaries and other factor payments: arrow D. Arrows A and B are the two sides of the product market; C and D are the two sides of the factor market.

The solid flows are money and run clockwise; the dashed flows are real things, goods and labor, and run the other way. Every dollar one group spends is income to the other, which is why the flow never stops.
