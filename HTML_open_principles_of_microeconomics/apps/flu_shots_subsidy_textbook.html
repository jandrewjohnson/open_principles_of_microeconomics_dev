The Market for Flu Shots with Spillover Benefits

Buyers count only their own protection, so the market stops short of the socially worthwhile number of shots; a subsidy equal to the spillover closes the gap

A. A Positive Externality and a Subsidy
Explanation

The market demand curve DMarket reflects only the marginal private benefit: what a vaccinated person gains from not catching flu. Supply is the marginal private cost of producing the shots. They meet at QMarket and PMarket.

But a vaccinated person also makes everyone around them less likely to catch flu, a benefit no buyer counts. Adding it gives the marginal social benefit, Ds, which lies above market demand, and the socially worthwhile quantity QSocial is larger than QMarket. A voucher worth exactly the spillover closes the gap: suppliers receive PSocial, buyers pay only PSubsidy, and the quantity rises to the socially optimal level. The slider sets how large the spillover benefit is.
